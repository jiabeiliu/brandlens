Store strategy is a central pillar of the turnaround:

- Smaller-format prototypes: new coffeehouse concepts with a smaller physical footprint opened in New York City and San Antonio. They feature a shortened counter, a compact food display, flexible seating, and a "folded bar" configuration that fits narrow spaces — adaptable to expensive urban real estate while keeping the inviting cafe experience. Six prototypes were operating as of late 2026.
- 1,000 store "uplifts": renovations blending global heritage with local relevance — less glare, more outlets, more comfortable seating — at about $150,000 each, done without closing stores, targeting completion by end of 2026. The first uplifted stores opened in New York and Southern California.
- Growth targets: after closing hundreds of less profitable stores, Starbucks expects up to 175 new U.S. stores in the current year and around 400 in 2028. CEO Brian Niccol says the company sees room for at least 5,000 new U.S. cafes long-term, with the smaller format — 20% cheaper to build, with seating, drive-thru and mobile pickup — unlocking thousands of sites, especially in the central, southern and northeastern U.S.
- Fiscal 2026 outlook: 440 net new store openings projected globally, down from an earlier 600-650 outlook, reflecting the disciplined "uplift + selective growth" approach.

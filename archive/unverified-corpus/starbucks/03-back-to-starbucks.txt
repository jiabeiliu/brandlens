"Back to Starbucks" is the turnaround strategy launched under CEO Brian Niccol, who joined in 2024. The diagnosis: Starbucks had spent years optimizing for speed — mobile ordering, pickup-focused store redesigns, hard stools, blocked power outlets — and in the process made cafes less desirable places to linger. Sales suffered from higher prices, longer pickup waits and lackluster food.

The fix, backed by more than $1 billion in investment:

- Bring back the "third place": comfortable seating, more power outlets, free refills, warmer design — making stores places people want to stay.
- Green Apron Service: $500 million invested in more staffing hours, larger teams and new in-store technology, aiming for the "world's greatest customer experience." The company reports record-low turnover and record-high shift completion.
- Simplify and refocus: closed hundreds of underperforming stores (about 1% of the 18,000+ North American footprint, ~$300 million restructuring cost) and exited mobile-only locations.
- Marketing reset: seasonal campaigns, brand collaborations, and the Starbucks Rewards loyalty program and mobile app remain core growth levers. CMO Tressie Lieberman has spoken about marketing's role in the turnaround.

Early signals cited by the company include record-breaking sales weeks tied to seasonal product lines and improving partner (employee) satisfaction.

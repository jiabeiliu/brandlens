Source: Nike marketing history (public knowledge, summarized Oct 2026).

Nike's marketing is built on athlete partnerships and cultural storytelling:

- Signature athletes: Nike has built signature lines around basketball icons including Michael Jordan (Jordan Brand), LeBron James, Kobe Bryant, Kevin Durant, and now Caitlin Clark, A'ja Wilson and Sabrina Ionescu in the women's game. The Caitlin 1 launch was described as one of the most anticipated signature drops since LeBron's in 2003.
- The "Just Do It" slogan debuted July 1, 1988, created by Wieden+Kennedy co-founder Dan Wieden. Advertising Age named it one of the top five ad slogans of the 20th century; it is enshrined in the Smithsonian Institution.
- Nike sponsors high-profile athletes and sports teams worldwide across basketball, soccer (national teams including Brazil, France, England; clubs across Europe), track and field, tennis and more. Its first professional team uniform was for the Portland Timbers soccer club in 1979.
- The 1976 "There is no finish line" brand ad — in which no Nike product was shown — established Nike's emotional, athlete-first advertising style.
- Digital and community: the SNKRS app drives hype for limited drops; Nike Run Club and Nike Training Club apps build daily engagement with runners and fitness consumers; run clubs double as social scenes that Nike actively cultivates.
- Retail experience: Niketown flagship stores and nike.com anchor direct-to-consumer, supported by membership programs that tie apps, retail and online together.
